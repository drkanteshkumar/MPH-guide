# Funding your MPH

## Why I wrote this

I applied to three MPH programs in the US and received scholarships from two: the Presidential Scholarship at Harvard Chan (100% of tuition) and the Dean's MPH Scholarship at Johns Hopkins Bloomberg.

The biggest thing I learned: every school handles funding differently. At Hopkins, I was considered automatically when I applied. At Harvard, I had to submit a separate scholarship application with its own deadline. If I had missed that form, I would not be at Harvard today.

So my one piece of advice is simple: apply for every scholarship you are eligible for. Something is always better than nothing.

## How MPH funding works

Most MPH funding is a partial or full tuition scholarship decided at admission. Four things decide what you get, and whether you get anything at all.

**1. Automatic vs separate application.** Some schools review everyone for scholarships using the admission application. Others need a separate form. Harvard Chan, for example, emails you after you submit SOPHAS (if you indicated interest in financial aid) with login details for its financial aid portal, MyFinaid, where you fill out a Grant/Scholarship Application. The deadline is usually January 8 with no late submissions, so start early and take your time with it ([Harvard Chan](https://hsph.harvard.edu/tuition-and-financial-aid/how-to-apply-for-financial-aid)). Miss it and you are out for the whole program.

**2. Priority deadlines.** Many schools give scholarships only to applicants who apply by an earlier priority deadline, not the final admission deadline. Applying early is free money.

**3. Tuition vs cost of attendance.** A full-tuition scholarship does not cover rent, food, health insurance, fees or flights. In a city like Boston, living costs can match tuition. Plan for this before you accept an offer.

**4. International eligibility.** Many school scholarships are open to international students, but US federal loans and FAFSA are not. Always check each award's eligibility line.

Offers are often final. Harvard Chan, for example, states that funding is set at admission for the program's duration with no appeals. So put your best application in the first time.

Note on Hopkins: some departments also offer a Master's Tuition Scholarship covering 75% of second-year tuition for students in good standing ([source](https://www.jhsph.edu/admissions/scholarships/)). Ask your department.

Sommer Scholars is the Bloomberg School's most competitive award and has covered full tuition plus a stipend ([JHU Public Health magazine](https://magazine.publichealth.jhu.edu/2005/next-generation-scholarship)); check the current terms on the school's scholarship page.

For any school not listed, start with its financial aid office. Almost every school of public health has one, with a page on its website listing the scholarships it offers. Search the school's site for "financial aid" or "tuition and funding", open that page, and answer three questions: Is review automatic? Is there a priority deadline? Are international students eligible?

## External scholarships for Pakistani and international applicants

Outside funding usually opens in January and closes by spring, often before you hear back from schools. Apply in parallel with your MPH applications, not after.

| Scholarship | What it covers | Timing | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Fulbright Pakistan (USEFP)](https://newsletter.usefp.org/2026/01/2027-fulbright-program-announced) | Tuition, textbooks, airfare, living stipend, health insurance | 2027 cycle closed April 1, 2026; the next cycle is usually announced early in the year | Clinical medicine is excluded; public health appears eligible, confirm in the official criteria |
| [Aga Khan Foundation International Scholarship Programme](https://urf.columbia.edu/fellowship/aga-khan-foundation-international-scholarships) | Tuition and living costs, as 50% grant and 50% loan | Forms open January 1 each year; deadline around March, local offices may set earlier dates | Pakistan is eligible; master's applicants get priority |

Also look at your employer, provincial government and professional associations. Smaller awards add up, and they rarely have many applicants.

## Assistantships, jobs and loans

Once you arrive, you can close part of the gap with paid work. These are not guaranteed, so do not count on them when deciding whether you can afford a program.

- **Teaching and research assistantships.** Paid roles as a teaching assistant or with a faculty research project. Some include partial tuition support. Start emailing faculty whose work matches yours in your first semester; TA roles are often posted before each term.
- **Internships and on-campus jobs.** Paid internships (summer roles or your practicum) and on-campus jobs help with living costs. Students on an F-1 visa can generally work on campus during the semester within hour limits; check your school's international office for current rules.
- **Loans, after admission.** International students cannot use US federal loans. Some schools offer a loan as part of your aid package once you are admitted: at Harvard Chan, my award letter included a Harvard Credit Union loan to cover the gap between my scholarship and the full cost of attendance. Private lenders such as [MPOWER Financing](https://www.mpowerfinancing.com/blog/introducing-mpower-the-essentials) lend without a cosigner or collateral, from US$2,001 to US$100,000 per year, with rates starting around 10% APR. Borrow only what you need and understand the repayment terms before signing.

## Checklist

Work through this in order, starting about a year before you plan to enroll.

- [ ] **Summer to early fall:** shortlist schools and open each one's tuition and funding page
- [ ] For each school, note: automatic or separate form, priority deadline, international eligibility
- [ ] Start your statement of purpose and request recommendation letters early; scholarship committees read them closely
- [ ] **By December 1:** submit to schools with early priority deadlines (for example Michigan)
- [ ] **Early January:** submit and verify remaining applications (Emory's merit deadline is January 5)
- [ ] **After submitting SOPHAS:** watch your inbox for separate scholarship forms (Harvard Chan's closes January 8)
- [ ] **January to March:** apply for external scholarships such as Fulbright and the Aga Khan Foundation
- [ ] **Spring:** compare offers on total cost (tuition plus living costs), not tuition alone
- [ ] Before accepting, confirm what each award covers and whether it renews for year two

## Before you rely on this

Funding rules, deadlines and award names change every admissions cycle. Everything here was checked on official pages as of October 2026, but always confirm on each school's own site before you apply.

Good luck. Apply for everything.
